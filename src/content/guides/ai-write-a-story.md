The short answer: open a chatbot such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot and tell it three things — who the story is for, what happens in it, and roughly how long it should be. It will write a first draft in seconds. Then you read it, tell it what to change, and keep going until it sounds like something you would be happy to share. You don't need any writing experience, and you don't need to type anything clever.

That's the whole method. The rest of this article shows what to type, how to stop the result sounding like a machine wrote it, and which stories are better left in your own words.

## What to type — three examples you can copy

The biggest mistake is asking for "a story" and nothing else. The AI then has to guess everything, and you get something bland. Describe the situation instead.

**A bedtime story for a grandchild:**

> "Write a short bedtime story for a five-year-old who loves trains and is a little afraid of the dark. A friendly engine called Bertie is the hero. Gentle, no frightening bits, about 300 words, and a happy ending where Bertie falls asleep."

**A story from your own life:**

> "I want to write down the summer I spent working at a seaside café when I was 18. I'll give you the facts in a moment. Please turn them into a warm, funny story of about 500 words, written in the first person. Don't add anything I haven't told you."

**A story just for fun:**

> "Write a gentle mystery set in a village post office. Someone has been leaving a different flower on the counter every Monday. Cosy, no violence, about 600 words."

Notice what each one has: a reader, a subject, a mood, a length, and sometimes a rule about what to leave out. If you have never used a chatbot before, [your first conversation with an AI](/guides/your-first-conversation/) walks through the very first step.

## How to make it sound like you

A first draft from an AI tends to be tidy and a little generic. That's normal. It has read a huge amount of writing and it aims for the average of it. [How does AI "know" things?](/guides/how-does-ai-know/) explains why. The good news is that you can steer it, simply by talking back.

Try lines like these:

- "Make it shorter and use plainer words."
- "Rewrite the first paragraph so it sounds like someone chatting, not reading from a book."
- "My grandson is called Oliver, not Tom. Change that everywhere."
- "The ending is too neat. Make it quieter."
- "Keep the second paragraph exactly as it is and change the rest."

Better still, paste in a few lines you have written yourself — a letter, a card, even an email — and say, "Write the story in a voice like this." It will copy the feel of your sentences. Then change any word that doesn't sound like you. The final version should be partly the machine's and mostly yours.

Read the story aloud once. If you stumble over a sentence, so will the listener. Ask the AI to fix that sentence, or fix it yourself.

## Where it goes wrong

An AI does not know your family, your village or your past. It only knows what you tell it, and when you leave gaps it fills them with something plausible. That causes a few predictable problems.

**It invents details.** Ask for your own story and it may add a name, a place or a feeling that never happened. In a made-up tale that's harmless. In a family history it is not. This is why the example above says "Don't add anything I haven't told you", and why you should still read every line against what you remember. There is more on this in [how to tell when AI is wrong](/guides/when-ai-is-wrong/).

**It repeats itself.** Look out for the same phrase or the same sort of ending turning up again and again. Ask it for something different.

**It can sound too polished.** A story that is smooth all the way through can feel empty. Your own small, odd details — the burnt toast, the neighbour's dog — are what make a story worth reading. Put them in.

**It may borrow.** Chatbots are built from existing writing, and the rules about who owns what they produce are still being settled. Sharing a story with your family is fine. If you plan to sell a story or publish it, check the current rules for the service you use, and don't assume anything.

## Stories you should keep for yourself

Some things are better written by you, or with a person beside you.

**Anything private.** Don't paste in names, addresses, health details or other people's secrets. What you type goes to a company's computers. [Is it safe to use AI?](/guides/is-it-safe/) covers this properly, and it is worth five minutes before you share anything personal.

**Words that carry real feeling.** A eulogy, a letter to someone who is ill, a wedding speech. The AI can help you find a way in — "Give me five ways to start a speech about my brother" — but the words that matter should be yours. People can tell, and it is your voice they want.

**Anything that must be true.** A story is allowed to be invented. A family history, a memorial or a historical account is not. Check every date and name against a real source or a relative.

## A routine that works

1. **Decide who it's for.** A child, a friend, yourself. This changes everything.
2. **Write two or three sentences about what happens.** Rough is fine.
3. **Ask for a draft, and say how long and what mood.**
4. **Read it aloud, then tell the AI what to change.** Do this two or three times.
5. **Put in your own details.** Names, places, a joke only your family would get.
6. **Check anything that is supposed to be true.** Then save it, print it or read it out.

Many people find it more fun to write the story together with a grandchild — the child chooses the hero, you type, the AI writes, and everyone laughs at the odd bits.

## The short version

- Tell the AI who the story is for, what happens, the mood and the length. Don't ask for "a story" alone.
- Keep talking to it: shorter, plainer, change the ending, change the name.
- Paste in something you've written so it copies your voice, then edit until it sounds like you.
- For true stories, tell it not to add anything, and check every detail yourself.
- Keep private information out, and keep the most heartfelt words for yourself.
