If you've been putting off understanding AI because every explanation seems to
assume you already understand it — you're in the right place. Let's start from
zero.

## The short answer

The AI everyone is talking about is a computer program that has **read an
enormous amount of text** — books, articles, websites — and learned the
patterns in how people write and explain things. When you ask it something, it
uses those patterns to write an answer, one word at a time.

That's it. No feelings, no intentions, no little person inside the machine.

## A helpful way to picture it

Imagine someone who has read nearly everything ever written, has a remarkable
memory for *how things are usually said* — but wasn't actually there for any of
it. They never saw a sunset or baked a loaf of bread; they've only read
millions of descriptions of both.

Ask them anything and they'll give you a fluent, confident, usually helpful
answer. But because they answer from patterns rather than lived experience,
they'll occasionally be confidently wrong — like a well-read friend who
sometimes mixes up the details. (That matters so much we wrote a whole guide
about it: [how to tell when AI is wrong](/guides/when-ai-is-wrong/).)

## The words you'll hear, translated

You don't need to memorise these. Come back to this list whenever a word
bothers you.

- **AI (artificial intelligence).** The umbrella term for computer programs
  that do things which normally require human thinking — writing, translating,
  answering questions.
- **Chatbot.** An AI you talk to by typing (or speaking), the way you'd text a
  person. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are the well-known ones.
- **Prompt.** A fancy word for *the thing you type to the AI*. Your question or
  request. That's all it means.
- **Model.** The "brain" behind a chatbot — the program that was trained on all
  that text. New models come out regularly, like new car models.
- **Hallucination.** The industry's odd word for when AI states something false
  as if it were true. It's not lying — it has no idea it's wrong.

## Why does it feel so human?

Because it learned from us. It has absorbed the rhythm of human conversation —
politeness, humour, encouragement — so talking to it *feels* like talking to
someone. It's worth remembering that the warmth is a pattern it learned, not a
feeling it has. You don't need to thank it or worry about bothering it (though
many of us say "please" anyway — no harm in that).

## What it's genuinely good at — and not

**Good at:** explaining things at whatever level you ask for, rewriting and
summarising text, drafting letters and emails, suggesting ideas, translating,
and patiently answering the questions you might feel silly asking a person.
Patience is its superpower: it will explain something ten times, ten different
ways, and never sigh.

**Not good at:** knowing what happened five minutes ago, doing precise
arithmetic, being right 100% of the time, and knowing *you* — your health, your
finances, your family — beyond what you tell it.

## The one idea to take with you

Treat AI like a **knowledgeable, tireless, occasionally mistaken helper** — not
an oracle. Use it to think *with* you, not *for* you. If you hold onto that one
idea, you're already using AI more wisely than many people who use it daily.
