Short answer: Google's AI has read an enormous amount of writing and learned the patterns in it. When you ask a question, it writes an answer one word at a time, choosing the words that are most likely to fit. It does not look the answer up in a book of facts, and it does not understand things the way you do.

That one idea explains almost everything about it: why it is so useful, and why it is sometimes wrong while sounding completely sure.

## Two different things called "Google"

Many people think "Google" is one machine. It is really two jobs, and the AI only does one of them.

**The search part** finds web pages. When you type a question, Google looks through its huge collection of websites and puts the most relevant ones in a list. This is the old, familiar Google. It points you to pages written by people.

**The AI part** writes. It reads your question, and often some of those web pages too, and then produces a short summary in its own words. You may see this as a box at the top of the results, often called an "AI Overview", or as a chat in Google's chatbot, [Gemini](/guides/gemini-explained/).

So when you see a neat paragraph above the list of links, no person wrote it. A computer program wrote it a moment ago, just for you.

## How the AI learned to write

Think of someone who has read more books, articles and websites than a hundred people could read in a lifetime. They do not remember each page. But they have a strong feel for how language goes. After "once upon a", they know what comes next. After "the capital of France is", they know that too.

Google's AI is trained in a similar way. It is shown huge amounts of text, over and over, and it practises guessing the next word. Each time it guesses wrong, it adjusts a little. After a very long time, it gets good at producing sentences that sound right and, very often, are right.

If you want the longer version, we explain it in [how AI "knows" things](/guides/how-does-ai-know/).

## What it does when you ask a question

Roughly, three things happen in a second or two:

1. **It reads your question.** It works out what you probably mean, even if you typed it clumsily or left out words.
2. **It gathers material.** For a summary in search, it can pull in text from pages Google has found. That is one reason the box often lists the websites it drew on.
3. **It writes an answer.** It builds the reply word by word, using the patterns it learned and the material it gathered.

Notice what is missing: a step where it checks the answer against the truth. It aims to sound like a good answer. Most of the time, that is also a correct answer. Sometimes it is not.

## Why it can be wrong

Because the AI is guessing what fits, it can make mistakes that look perfectly polished. It might:

- Mix up two similar things, such as two medicines with similar names.
- Repeat a mistake from a web page it read.
- Miss that something has changed since it was trained.
- Answer confidently when the honest reply would be "I am not sure".

None of this means it is broken. It is how this kind of tool works. We go through it properly in [when AI is wrong](/guides/when-ai-is-wrong/).

## How to use it sensibly

Here is a simple rule: **use the summary to get started, and use the websites to be sure.**

- **For everyday curiosity**, such as how a bird migrates or what a word means, the summary is usually fine.
- **For health, money, the law or anything with a deadline**, scroll down past the summary to a trusted website, such as your national health service or your bank. Better still, speak to a real person: your doctor, your pharmacist, your bank.
- **If the answer surprises you**, check it. Surprising answers are the ones most worth a second look.
- **Look at the sources.** If the summary shows which pages it used, open one and see whether it really says that.

You can also ask it to help you check. In the chatbot, try: *"What would I need to double-check in your answer?"* It will often point to the weak spots.

## What Google can see

Google's AI works inside your Google account, so it is fair to ask what it knows about you. The search itself is the same as it always was: Google can see what you search for, as it could before. The chatbot also keeps your chats, unless you change the setting.

Some versions can connect to your email or calendar, but only if you allow it. It is a setting, not something that happens by itself. Look through the privacy settings in your Google account if you want to see what is switched on, and keep the same habits as with any AI: [no passwords or ID numbers](/guides/never-type-this/).

## You do not have to use it

The AI summary is there whether you asked for it or not, and that can feel pushy. You can simply scroll past it. The normal list of websites is still underneath, and it works as it always did.

## The short version

- Google's AI writes answers by guessing the most fitting words, based on patterns it learned from a vast amount of text.
- It is not looking up a book of facts, so it can sound sure and still be wrong.
- Use the summary to get started, and a trusted website or a real person to be sure.
- For health, money and legal questions, always check with a human.
- You can ignore the AI box at any time. The ordinary search results are still there.
