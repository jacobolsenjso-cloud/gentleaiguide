It's a reasonable thing to wonder. You've been chatting with this thing for weeks. Does it *know* you now? Is there a growing file somewhere with your name on it? And if so — where's the bin?

Here are the honest answers, followed by the three settings worth five minutes of your time.

## Three different kinds of "remembering"

People use "remember" to mean three things, and they work differently.

**1. Within one conversation.** Yes. As long as you're in the same chat, it holds everything you've said. Ask a follow-up, refer back to "that letter" — it knows. Start a *new* chat and, by default, that's gone. Each chat is its own bubble.

**2. Across conversations ("memory").** Some chatbots now have a memory feature: they note things about you — "prefers short answers," "has a dog called Bramble" — and use them in future chats. This is optional, it's usually on by default in newer versions, and it's the one people mean when they feel watched. You can see what it has noted and delete any of it. More below.

**3. The company's records.** Separately from what the chatbot "remembers," the company stores your conversations on its computers. That's how your chat history appears on the left. They may keep copies for a while even after you delete them, and — unless you turn it off — they may use your conversations to improve future versions of the system. This isn't a person reading your chats; it's a machine learning from millions of them. But it's still your words, stored.

## What it's *not* doing

It's not listening through your microphone when you're not using it. It's not reading your other tabs, your emails or your files. It's not connected to your bank. It cannot see you. Everything it knows about you, you typed (or the memory feature noted from what you typed).

## The three settings worth changing today

Every chatbot has these, in slightly different places. The names below are the ones used at the time of writing; if they've moved, search the settings for "data" or "memory."

**Setting 1: Stop your chats being used for training.**
- *ChatGPT:* your name (bottom left) → *Settings* → *Data controls* → turn off *"Improve the model for everyone."*
- *Gemini:* gemini.google.com → *Settings and help* → *Activity* → turn off *Keep activity* (older versions call it *Gemini Apps Activity*).
- *Copilot:* *Settings* → *Privacy* → *Model training* → off.

Your chats still work exactly the same. They're just not used to teach the next version.

**Setting 2: See and prune the memory.**
- *ChatGPT:* *Settings* → *Personalisation* → *Memory* → *Manage*. You'll see a list of things it has noted. Delete anything you like, or turn memory off entirely.
- *Gemini:* *Settings* → *Personal context* → turn off. (This is separate from *Keep activity*, above, which controls whether your chats are stored.)
- *Copilot:* *Settings* → *Privacy* → *Personalisation*.

Read that list once. It's oddly revealing — and reassuringly mundane.

**Setting 3: Delete old conversations.**
In the list of chats on the left, hover over one → the three dots or a bin icon → *Delete*. To delete everything at once: *Settings* → *Data controls* → *Delete all chats* (ChatGPT), or the equivalent. Deleted chats are usually removed from the company's systems within about a month.

## If you want to leave entirely

You can delete your account. It's under *Settings* → *Data controls* or *Account* → *Delete account*. Everything goes. There's usually a short waiting period in case you change your mind.

You don't need a reason. Some people prefer to use these tools without an account at all — ChatGPT allows that for basic use — and accept that nothing is saved between visits. That's a perfectly good way to live.

## Two habits that make all of this simpler

**Assume it's stored.** Not read, not shared — but stored. That single assumption tells you what to leave out. [Here's the short list.](/guides/never-type-this/)

**Have a look at the settings once a year.** Companies change defaults. What was off may now be on. Five minutes, once a year, and you stay in charge.

## The short version

Within a chat, it remembers. Between chats, only if the memory feature is on — and you can see and delete what it's noted. The company stores your conversations and may use them for training unless you switch that off. Three settings, five minutes, and you've closed every door you'd want closed.
