You can try an AI chatbot today, for free, without installing anything or
giving out personal information. This guide walks you through it at a
comfortable pace. Take your time — there is no way to break anything.

## Before we start, three reassurances

1. **You cannot damage anything.** Not your computer, not the AI. The worst
   that can happen is an unhelpful answer.
2. **There are no stupid questions.** The AI doesn't judge, doesn't gossip, and
   doesn't get impatient. Ask it what you'd hesitate to ask a person.
3. **You can leave at any time.** Close the page and it's over. Nothing keeps
   running.

## Step 1: Open a chatbot

The three best-known chatbots are ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. They work the
same way for our purposes — like different brands of car, all with a steering
wheel where you'd expect it.

In your web browser (the program you're reading this in), go to one of these
addresses by typing it in the address bar at the very top of the window:

- **chatgpt.com** — ChatGPT
- **claude.ai** — Claude
- **gemini.google.com** — Gemini

Some of these may ask you to create a free account (an email address and a
password). They ask so they can remember your conversations for you. If a page
asks you to *pay*, look for the words "free", "skip" or "maybe later" — you do
not need to pay for anything in this guide.

## Step 2: Find the box and say hello

Every chatbot has one thing in common: **a text box**, usually at the bottom of
the page, often with faint text like "Ask anything" inside it. Click in the box
and type:

> Hello! I'm brand new to this. Can you explain, in one short paragraph, what I
> can use you for?

Press the **Enter** key (or the arrow button next to the box). Within a few
seconds, the AI will write back. That's it — you're having a conversation with
an AI.

## Step 3: Have a real conversation

Here's the part nobody tells beginners: **the reply is a conversation, not a
verdict.** If the answer is too long, too technical, or misses the point, just
say so — the way you would with a person:

> That was too complicated. Can you say it more simply?

> Shorter, please.

> That's not quite what I meant. I meant…

The AI remembers what was said earlier in the conversation, so you never need
to start over. Asking follow-up questions isn't cheating — it's the whole
skill.

## Step 4: Try something useful to you

Now try one of these, or invent your own. Write casually, as if to a friendly
neighbour — full sentences, typos and all. It copes fine.

> Write a short, warm birthday message for my granddaughter who is turning 12
> and loves horses.

> I have chicken, rice and a bag of spinach. What can I make for dinner?

> Explain what "streaming" means, for someone who grew up with a video rental
> shop.

## If something goes wrong

- **The answer stops halfway.** Type "please continue" and it will.
- **You lost the page.** Go back to the same address; if you made an account,
  your conversation is usually saved there.
- **It says it can't help with something.** That's normal — chatbots have
  rules. Rephrase, or ask it why.

You now know 90% of what daily AI users do: open the page, type in the box,
read the reply, and ask again. The remaining 10% is judgement — knowing what to
