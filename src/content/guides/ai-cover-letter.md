You can use AI to write a cover letter by giving it two things: the job advert, and a plain list of what you have actually done. Ask it for a first draft, then read it aloud, change anything that doesn't sound like you, and check every claim is true. The AI gets you past the blank page. You make it honest and personal.

If you have not worked with an AI chatbot before, [what AI actually is](/guides/what-is-ai/) and [your first conversation](/guides/your-first-conversation/) will take ten minutes and make the rest of this easier.

## Why a cover letter is a good job for AI

A cover letter has a fairly fixed shape: why you are writing, why you suit the job, and what you would like to happen next. Most people find the shape easy and the first sentence impossible. That is exactly where AI helps.

It is also a low-risk task. You will read the result before anyone else does, and you know the facts better than the AI ever will. If you have been out of the job market for a while, or you are changing direction later in life, a draft to react to can take away a lot of the dread.

## What to give it

The quality of the letter depends almost entirely on what you hand over. Gather these before you start:

- **The job advert.** Copy and paste the whole thing.
- **Your experience, in your own rough words.** Not a polished CV. Just a list of what you have done, for example: "Ran a small shop for eleven years. Trained new staff. Did the accounts myself."
- **Anything the advert asks for that you are unsure about.** Tell the AI honestly where you have gaps.
- **The tone you want.** Warm and professional is usually right.

Here is a request you can copy and change:

> "I am applying for the job below. Please write a cover letter of about 250 words. Use only the experience I list here and do not add anything I haven't mentioned. Warm, plain, professional tone. British spelling.
>
> Job advert: [paste it here]
>
> My experience: [paste your list here]"

The sentence "use only the experience I list here" matters. It is the best protection against the next problem.

## Check what it wrote before you send anything

AI sometimes invents things to make a letter sound impressive. It may add a qualification you don't have, a number you never mentioned, or a claim that you "led a team of twenty". It does this smoothly, which is why it is easy to miss. [How to tell when AI is wrong](/guides/when-ai-is-wrong/) explains why this happens.

Go through the draft slowly with these questions:

1. **Is every fact true?** Dates, job titles, skills, results. Delete anything you can't prove.
2. **Is the company name right?** Is the job title right? Mistakes here are the quickest way to look careless.
3. **Does it sound like you?** Read it aloud. If you would never say a sentence to a person's face, change it.
4. **Does it say anything real?** Phrases like "I am a passionate team player" say nothing. Replace them with one small, true example.

## Make it yours

The most common complaint from people who read lots of applications is that the letters all sound the same. AI makes that worse if you send its first draft untouched.

Two easy fixes. First, ask for changes in plain language: "Make it shorter", "Make the opening less formal", "Mention my years of customer service earlier." Second, add one sentence yourself that only you could write, such as why this particular job or company appeals to you.

Some employers say they don't mind AI help, and some ask applicants not to use it. If the advert says anything about this, follow it. If you are unsure, it is fine to ask. You are still the author of the letter if the experience and the final words are yours.

## A short example of improving a draft

Say the first draft opens with: "I am writing to express my enthusiastic interest in the position." It is correct, but it could be anyone. You can reply to the AI: "Rewrite the opening in two plain sentences. Say that I ran a shop for eleven years and enjoy helping customers." Now the letter starts with something real.

You can keep going like this, one small request at a time. Ask it to cut a paragraph, to explain a word you don't understand, or to suggest three different openings so you can pick the one you like. Nothing is final until you copy it out and send it, so there is no harm in asking the same thing several times.

When you are happy with it, save a copy of your own version. Next time you apply for a job, you can paste it in and say "Adapt this letter for the new advert below." That saves most of the work for every application after the first.

## Look after your personal details

Chatbots are not private in the way a letter in a sealed envelope is. Leave out your date of birth, home address, phone number, national insurance number and anything about your health. You can add your contact details to the finished letter yourself. [What never to type into an AI](/guides/never-type-this/) has a short list you can keep beside the computer.

## The short version

- Give the AI the job advert and your real experience, in your own rough words.
- Tell it to use only what you have listed.
- Check every fact, name and date before sending.
- Rewrite until it sounds like you, and add one sentence only you could write.
- Keep personal details out of the chat, and add them to the finished letter yourself.
