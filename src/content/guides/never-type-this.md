Here is the reassuring part first: the vast majority of what you'd ever type into a chatbot is perfectly fine. Recipes, letters, questions about history, help with a crossword, "why does my knee click" — go ahead.

But there is a short list of things that should never go in. Not because the chatbot is evil, but because [what you type is stored somewhere](/guides/is-it-safe/), and stored things can leak, be read by staff, or be used to train future versions. Treat a chatbot the way you'd treat a helpful stranger at a library desk: happy to help, but not someone you hand your wallet to.

Print this list. Stick it by the computer. That's the whole job.

## Never type these

**1. Passwords.** Ever. For anything. There is no situation where a chatbot needs your password to help you. If you want help *choosing* a good password, ask for advice — don't type the one you have.

**2. Your ID or account numbers.** National ID / social security number, passport number, driving licence number, bank account and card numbers, PIN codes. The chatbot can't do anything useful with these, and they're exactly what a thief would want.

**3. Other people's private information.** Your friend's medical situation, your grandson's school problems, a colleague's home address. It's not yours to share, and they didn't agree to it. Describe the situation without the name if you need advice.

**4. Anything you'd be embarrassed to see printed.** A safe test: imagine this conversation on a noticeboard with your name on it. If that makes you wince, don't type it. Conversations are rarely made public — but "rarely" isn't "never," and you can't take it back.

**5. Full documents with personal details still in them.** A letter from the bank, a medical report, a contract. It's fine to paste these *after* you've blanked out the names, numbers and addresses — see the "what to type instead" section below.

## Be careful with these

**Your full name plus your address.** Either on its own is usually fine. Both together, with your date of birth, is enough to impersonate you. Keep them apart.

**Health questions about yourself.** Asking "what could cause dizziness" is fine. Typing your full history with dates, diagnoses and medications is more than the chatbot needs — and more than you want stored. Keep it general, and take the answer to a real doctor. ([More on AI and health questions.](/guides/ai-and-my-doctor/))

**Money details.** "How should I think about a pension?" is fine. "I have exactly this much in these accounts" is not necessary and not wise.

**Photos of documents or people.** Some chatbots let you upload pictures. A photo of a plant or a recipe is fine. A photo of your passport, your card, or someone who hasn't agreed to it — no.

## What to type instead

The trick is to remove the *identifying* parts and keep the *situation*. The chatbot doesn't need to know who you are to help you.

| Instead of… | Type… |
|---|---|
| "I'm Margaret Holm, born 12/3/1958, living at 14 Elm Road…" | "I'm a woman in my late sixties…" |
| Pasting the whole bank letter | Pasting it with the name, account number and address replaced by "[name]", "[number]", "[address]" |
| "My neighbour John Smith has dementia and…" | "A neighbour of mine has memory problems and…" |
| "My password is Fluffy1962, is it strong?" | "Is a pet's name plus a year a strong password?" (It isn't.) |

You'll find the answers are just as good. Often better — because you've had to describe the problem clearly.

## If you already have

You may be reading this and thinking of something you typed last month. Don't panic; most of these are low-risk in practice. But do three things:

1. **If it was a password, change it.** Today. On the real site, typed in yourself.
2. **Delete the conversation.** Most chatbots let you delete individual chats from the history list on the left. It won't undo everything (the company may keep a copy for a while), but it's the right first step.
3. **Turn off "improve the model" if you can.** In ChatGPT, Gemini and others there's a setting that stops your chats being used for training. It's usually under Settings → Data controls, or similar. ([Full walkthrough here.](/guides/can-ai-remember-me/))

## The list, for the fridge

**Never:** passwords · ID and account numbers · other people's private details · anything you'd hate to see printed · documents with personal details left in.

**Careful:** full name + address together · detailed health history · exact money figures · photos of documents or people.

**The test:** would I say this to a helpful stranger at a library desk? If yes, type away.
