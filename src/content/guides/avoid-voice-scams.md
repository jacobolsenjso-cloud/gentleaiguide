The best way to avoid an AI voice scam is simple: **if a call asks for money or secrets, hang up and call the person back on a number you already have.** Do not use the number that just rang you. A real family member will understand. A scammer cannot answer your call-back.

That one habit beats almost every voice scam, because it does not depend on whether you can tell the voice is fake. Often you can't. This article explains why, and gives you a few more habits to go with it.

## What an AI voice scam actually is

Computers can now learn to copy the sound of a person's voice from a fairly short recording. This is often called *voice cloning*. The recording might come from a video on social media, a voicemail greeting, or a clip someone posted publicly.

A scammer then uses the copied voice on the phone. The call might sound like your grandson, your daughter or an old friend. The voice is upset. Something has gone wrong, and they need money quickly.

It helps to know what this does *not* mean. The scammer usually does not know your family well. They often know only a name and a phone number, and have found a recording to match. The cloned voice is a costume, not proof that anyone has been spying on you.

At the time of writing, these calls are still far less common than ordinary scam calls. But they are real, and the same old tricks work better when the voice sounds familiar.

## How the call usually goes

The details change, but the shape is nearly always the same:

- **Something frightening has happened.** An accident, an arrest, a hospital stay, a stolen bag abroad.
- **It is urgent.** "I need it right now. Please don't wait."
- **It is secret.** "Don't tell Mum." "Don't tell anyone, I'm so embarrassed."
- **Money must move in an unusual way.** Gift cards, a bank transfer to a stranger, cash handed to a courier, or a cryptocurrency payment.

Sometimes a second person joins the call, pretending to be a police officer, a lawyer or a doctor. This is meant to make it feel official and to keep you from stopping to think.

The voice itself may sound slightly flat, or oddly clean, or the person may not answer personal questions well. Do not rely on this. A good clone can fool a parent, and a bad phone line can make a real person sound strange.

## What to do during the call

Stay calm and slow things down. Scammers need you to rush, so the pause is your strongest tool.

1. **Say you will call back.** For example: "I'm going to hang up and ring you straight back."
2. **Hang up.** Do not stay on the line "just to check". Do not press any buttons they suggest.
3. **Call the person on the number you already have** in your phone or address book. If they are fine, you have lost nothing but two minutes.
4. **If you cannot reach them,** ring another family member or a friend who might know where they are.
5. **Do not send money** until you have spoken to the real person on a number you trust.

If the caller says they are from the police, a bank or a hospital, do the same. Hang up and look up the official number yourself, then ring that. Do not use a number the caller gives you.

## Habits to set up before anything happens

These cost nothing and take a few minutes, ideally with your family around the table.

**Agree a family safe word.** Choose a silly, private word or phrase that is never posted online. If anyone rings in trouble and asks for money, they must say it. A cloned voice will not know it. Change it if it is ever said somewhere others could hear.

**Ask a question only the real person could answer.** Not "what's your birthday", which can be found online. Something like "what did we eat last time you visited?" is better. Still, the call-back is safer than any quiz.

**Be careful what you post.** Short videos with clear speech are what cloning feeds on. You do not need to stop sharing, but think about who can see your posts. Setting a social media account to friends only is a sensible step.

**Talk to the people you love about this.** Many victims are caught because they felt too embarrassed to say anything. Tell your family that if anyone rings asking for secret money, you will always check first, and that they should do the same with you.

## If you have already sent money

It happens to careful people, and it is not your fault. Act quickly:

- Phone your bank straight away, using the number on your card or statement. Tell them it was a scam. The sooner they know, the more they may be able to do.
- Report it to the police or your country's fraud reporting service.
- Tell a family member. You do not have to carry this alone.

Do not pay anyone who contacts you afterwards offering to "get your money back". That is usually a second scam.

## The short version

- If a call asks for money or secrets, hang up and call back on a number you already have.
- Urgency plus secrecy plus unusual payment is the pattern. Slow down.
- A voice that sounds right proves nothing any more.
- Agree a family safe word, and keep it private.
- Tell someone, whether you were fooled or just nearly fooled.
